Little Dog Rescue: website audit and redesign brief

Audited September 7, 2026 (Pacific time). Public, read-only inspection. No applications, contact messages or payments submitted. This is a UX/technical review, not a penetration test, legal opinion, nonprofit verification or administrative security certification.

Executive conclusion

Keep Squarespace 7.1 and Shelterluv. Redesign the existing pages; do not rebuild the operational backend. The biggest opportunities are a clearer mobile hierarchy, shorter adoption guidance, trustworthy policy pages and consistent contact/donation paths. The existing dog search is functional. A new database or custom search service is unnecessary for the first phase.

Priority fixes before a visual relaunch: broken homepage donation link; empty policy pages; mobile photo behavior reported by the user; incomplete email-link formatting; legacy pages in the sitemap. Start with the Clear & Kind concept if retaining the familiar teal identity is the priority. The other two concepts offer meaningfully different visual directions without changing the platform.

Verified platform and ownership boundaries

  • Website: Squarespace 7.1. Public configuration reports templateVersion: "7.1", template ID 5c5a519771c10ba3470d8101, Squarespace server headers and Fluid Engine component assets. This is not WordPress. The original starter-template marketing name and subscription tier were not established; neither is needed to begin a 7.1 redesign.
  • Listings and profiles: Shelterluv, loaded through https://new.shelterluv.com/misc/shelterluv_embed.js, organization GID 47562, wrapper shelterluv_wrap_1737142902. The source uses EmbedAvailablePets with a one-column argument. Dog records and published photos belong to this workflow.
  • Adoption application: Shelterluv, https://www.shelterluv.com/matchme/adopt/LDR/Dog. Its first page rendered with required contact fields and an animal-interest field.
  • Contact: a JavaScript-rendered Squarespace form with name, email, subject and message, an optional newsletter checkbox and reCAPTCHA Enterprise loaded. The raw HTML extraction did not show this form, but browser rendering did. Do not describe it as absent.
  • Giving: the support page links to Squarespace's /donate-1 catalog and PayPal's charity page, plus Amazon wishlist and email options. Exact payment processor configuration, completed payment handling and receipts were not verified.
  • Accessibility helper: UserWay is present. Its presence does not establish accessibility conformance.
  • Existing design credit: footer credits Esperluette Creative. Coordinate handoff with the rescue and its current maintainer rather than assuming no one maintains the site.

Site styles, text/image/button/accordion blocks and page layouts can deliver most of the redesign. Squarespace parent CSS cannot reach into Shelterluv's cross-origin document. Frame width and surrounding spacing are site-level work; its gallery/search UI requires supported Shelterluv settings or vendor-side changes. API-based custom cards are possible as a separate engineering project, but are deliberately excluded from this redesign.

Scope and evidence

Inspected home, adoption, dog directory, supplied Valance deep link, foster, volunteer, support, about, contact, privacy, terms, accessibility, sitemap and robots resources. Followed the actual /contact-us navigation destination; guessed /contact and /our-mission return 404 but are not counted as broken navigation. Checked /donate because the homepage actually links there. Sampled /our-mission-old, /meet-our-dogs-new and /donate-1.

Browser: Chromium, 390px mobile and 1440px desktop for selected current pages. Checked dog search, empty results, opening filters and opening a photo. WebKit was not installed; actual iPhone/Safari behavior remains unverified. Public page HTML, headers, text, frame metadata and screenshots are retained in the local evidence folder. Timing readings are single, unthrottled lab samples, not Core Web Vitals or a measured conversion funnel.

What works

  1. The current navigation exposes the main adoption, fostering, volunteering, support and contact paths.
  2. HTTPS loads successfully. HTTP and the non-www hostname redirected to canonical HTTPS/www in the test.
  3. The dog directory rendered 27 dog names in the captured sample. Name search for Valance returned Valance; an impossible query produced a clear empty-results message. Clearing the query is available. Filters opened with sex, age group, breed, size and sort controls. Every combination was not tested.
  4. The supplied dog deep link resolved to Valance, LDR-A-1365, with photos, descriptive foster notes, weight, age, fee and location. A photo-enlargement control opened successfully. Full keyboard behavior and application preselection were not established.
  5. The adoption page explains application, review, conversation, matching, meeting and finalization. Important information exists; it is the hierarchy and repetition that need work.
  6. The contact form rendered and the page sets an expectation of a reply taking a few days. Its delivery route, spam handling and confirmation emails need a later owner-authorized end-to-end test.
  7. Policy links exist and page titles resolve, although their substantive contents are missing.

Prioritized findings

1. HIGH · Broken homepage donation path

Evidence: the homepage's “Donate Today” anchor targets /donate; GET returned 404. This does not mean every donation link is broken: header/support routes differ.

Change: point this CTA to /support-us or the approved giving destination. Add a /donate redirect after checking historical links. Verify the exact homepage click and the existing donation checkout/receipt flow with the owner. Owner: Squarespace editor. Effort: small.

2. HIGH · Policy pages have no substantive content

Evidence: /privacy-policy, /terms-and-conditions and /accessibility-statement returned 200 but both extracted and browser-rendered bodies contained only the heading and shared header/footer.

Change: have the organization approve accurate policy content covering its actual contact, application, newsletter, donation and third-party practices. Publish real accessibility contact/support information. Do not insert a generic promise of compliance. This is a trust/content defect, not evidence of a breach or a legal finding. Owner: rescue + site maintainer. Effort: medium, dependent on organization input.

3. HIGH · Mobile photo defect reported; exact cause not yet reproduced

Evidence: the user's two iPhone screenshots show stretched thumbnail strips, inconsistent gallery scale and clipped-looking CTAs. The current Chromium profile used 52×52 thumbnail boxes and a responsive frame; the exact distortion did not reproduce. No top-level horizontal overflow was measured at 390px.

Change: reproduce on the affected iPhone/Safari version, including direct deep-link entry, back navigation, pinch zoom and landscape. Check Squarespace mobile block width separately from Shelterluv's gallery. Test current supported embed configuration with Shelterluv before changing it. Supply both old and current evidence to vendor support if distortion is inside the frame. Do not promise a parent-CSS fix. Owner: site maintainer + Shelterluv. Effort: investigation required.

4. MEDIUM · Large decorative sections compete with the directory

Evidence: the directory begins with a photo masthead and large introductory title, then continues after the listing into a quote and repeated ways-to-help sections. The profile deep link inherits the entire surrounding page.

Change: use a compact directory heading, process/application links, the full-width supported embed and a short fallback/help block. Move long generic promotional modules to the homepage/support page. Owner: Squarespace editor. Effort: small–medium.

5. MEDIUM · Adoption guidance is long and repetitive

Evidence: the process appears as explanatory questions, a step list and narrative prose, followed by many testimonials and repeated donation/help content. The application-first condition is inside the longer explanation.

Change: lead with four readable stages, then fees/care/timing FAQ. Put the application-first condition next to the CTA. Preserve policy meaning; do not invent eligibility, approval time, guarantees, service geography or fees. Owner: rescue + editor. Effort: medium.

6. MEDIUM · Contact routing can reduce avoidable inquiries

Evidence: the contact page has a rendered general form but does not put a prominent email fallback next to the response-time note. The email and Seattle address are in the shared footer.

Change: show adoption/application guidance, a visible email fallback and general contact form together. Keep newsletter opt-in separate and unchecked unless verified otherwise. Explain whether the address is mailing-only or visitable after confirming with staff. A later implementation should retain the native form; previews intentionally omit a fake working submission. Owner: rescue + editor. Effort: small.

7. MEDIUM · Footer mailto is malformed-looking

Evidence: anchor contains mailto:SaveDogs@LittleDogRescue.org followed by a nonbreaking space and ?. The displayed email is readable. Client-specific failure has not been reproduced.

Change: normalize to mailto:SaveDogs@LittleDogRescue.org, then test on iPhone and a desktop mail client. Owner: editor. Effort: small.

8. MEDIUM · Legacy/duplicate pages remain discoverable

Evidence: sitemap has 32 URL entries, including six -old pages, /meet-our-dogs-new, /thank-you, /404 and the donation catalog. Sampled old mission and new dog pages return 200. Sitemap inclusion proves discoverability, not that Google has indexed them.

Change: inventory historic URLs before removing anything. Map replaced content to the correct current page with 301s; noindex legitimate utility pages where appropriate. Keep real donation product pages that still serve a purpose. Verify internal links and sitemap afterward. Owner: editor/SEO maintainer. Effort: medium.

9. MEDIUM · Search can be surfaced better; it does not need replacement

Evidence: existing Shelterluv search and empty state worked. Filters are in the embedded UI. The source passes one column; desktop configuration should be reviewed rather than assuming a new app is required.

Change: retain vendor search, give it more useful space, explain filters briefly and keep a direct official listing fallback if the embed fails. Confirm supported column/filter options with Shelterluv. Do not advertise compatibility filters such as “good with kids” unless they are supported by maintained data. Owner: editor + Shelterluv administrator. Effort: small–medium.

10. MEDIUM · Image presentation and authenticity need a consistent policy

Evidence: Squarespace pages mix public rescue/foster photos with filenames identifying Vecteezy stock imagery; source URLs request up to 2500w, though Squarespace may select responsive sizes at runtime. The featured dog's public profile contains eight photos. Lazy images below the fold can have unloaded sources and must not be falsely counted as broken.

Change: prefer permission-cleared real rescue photos in key adoption sections; preserve proportions; select deliberate focal points; use a consistent card crop without stretching; retain uncropped full-photo viewing where supported. Add useful alternative descriptions to meaningful images and empty alt only for decoration. Compress uploads and let Squarespace serve responsive renditions. Owner: photo/content editor. Effort: medium.

11. MEDIUM · Accessibility needs native fixes

Evidence: raw homepage HTML has seven images lacking an alt attribute; the runtime dog frame has no title. Context is needed to decide which images are decorative. UserWay and a skip link are present; they do not prove the site meets WCAG. Light teal text over white and text over photographs warrant a measured contrast pass.

Change: give the frame an accessible title, verify semantic headings and image purpose, provide visible focus and generous touch targets, measure contrast across actual states, and test zoom, keyboard, screen reader and gallery behavior. Do not purchase a replacement overlay as the first fix. Owner: editor + vendor for frame internals. Effort: medium.

12. MEDIUM · Metadata is incomplete on important pages

Evidence: dog directory and support page meta descriptions are empty; homepage description and canonical are present. Fragment-only dog routes do not provide separate top-level Squarespace metadata per dog.

Change: write specific page descriptions and social previews; check canonical URLs and approved nonprofit/site identity markup. Preserve dog-sharing links. Individual share-card/SEO improvements inside Shelterluv need vendor confirmation, not a promise that Squarespace CSS will fix them. Owner: editor. Effort: small–medium.

13. MEDIUM · Trust claims need dates and supporting evidence

Evidence: repeated copy claims over 1,600 dogs helped annually, around 20 in care and unusually strong adoption success. The captured directory listed 27 names; this does not prove the approximate roster claim false, but it shows static counts age easily.

Change: date impact statistics and have the rescue substantiate them; remove absolute matching/success promises. Display approved legal identity and a verified nonprofit-record link if supplied. Do not infer nonprofit status from the footer alone. Owner: rescue. Effort: small once facts are available.

14. LOW · Content polish and navigation consistency

Evidence: volunteer H1 says “Resuce”; footer repeats “Rescue Rescue” and shows 2025. Wishlist copy talks about one-time/monthly gifts instead of requested supplies. Several social links use HTTP URLs.

Change: correct typos, approve current footer text, describe wishlist supplies accurately and normalize supported social destinations to HTTPS. Keep button text short and sentence-case where possible. Owner: editor. Effort: small.

Public security and privacy review

Observed positives: HTTPS verified through normal certificate-validating clients; HTTP→HTTPS and apex→www 301s; HSTS max-age=15552000; X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff; X-Frame-Options present; contact/application pages load reCAPTCHA services. These are useful controls, not proof of total security or bot resistance.

Hardening opportunities: no Content-Security-Policy, Referrer-Policy or Permissions-Policy response header was observed in sampled page headers. Some responses repeated SAMEORIGIN in X-Frame-Options. These are platform-level observations, not automatically exploitable vulnerabilities. Ask Squarespace about supported controls; do not place a reverse proxy in front of their live site just to satisfy a header checklist. Any CSP must be tested against Squarespace, Shelterluv, forms, payments and accessibility services before enforcement.

Third-party boundaries: Squarespace, Shelterluv, UserWay, Google reCAPTCHA, social integrations and payment/donation destinations appear in the flow. Inventory actual cookies/storage and purposes after reviewing administrative settings. The privacy page currently does not explain these practices. Loading a CAPTCHA is not proof its server-side verification is configured correctly.

Not assessed: account MFA and roles, recovery ownership, DNS/registrar administration, private records, retention, backup/export reliability, mail delivery authentication, form storage routing, spam/abuse rates, payment settlement, API scopes, patch management or compromise indicators. No vulnerability scanning, credential probing, exploit attempts or form spam was performed. Follow up with authorized owner access, not guesses.

Performance

Single Chromium initial-load samples: homepage 89 resource entries; directory 84. The directory's top-frame timing showed roughly 0.61s DOMContentLoaded and 1.88s load in this local network sample. Cross-origin frames, cache and timing restrictions mean recorded transfer bytes are not a complete page-weight measure. These are not mobile 4G results, Lighthouse scores, field CWV, or evidence of a conversion rate.

Reduce duplicated long sections, select appropriately sized genuine photos, avoid decorative animation and unnecessary social/widget work above the primary CTA. Establish a repeatable cold-cache phone/network baseline after design selection. Measure LCP, CLS and INP using field data if the rescue has access. Do not migrate platforms based on one fast or slow lab reading.

Full interface-polish review

Scope: public Squarespace pages and observed Shelterluv frame; proposed plain HTML/CSS review site. Typography: hierarchy, wrapping and prose density inspected. Surfaces: image proportions, frame spacing, button sizes and card geometry inspected. Animations: no authored entrance sequences in the prototypes; reduced-motion preference supported. Existing site's full motion timeline was not reviewed at 10% speed. Icons: preview uses text/heart mark and directional arrows, with decorative symbols hidden where appropriate. Performance: public resource sample and local asset/layout checks, not a full performance profile.

Proposals map to findings 3–6 and 9–14. The concepts use more restrained headings, stronger color contrast choices, consistent images, larger controls, visible focus, responsive contact layouts and simpler page length. Borders communicate groups; shadows and animation are not used as substitutes for hierarchy.

Considered and rejected: a custom dog-database frontend (maintenance beyond a design refresh); a new WordPress build (unnecessary migration); a carousel as the homepage hero (less direct than one genuine photo); a new accessibility overlay (does not repair underlying semantics).

Live-site verdict: Needs changes; hold a relaunch until the high-priority defects are resolved. Exact iPhone photo failure, full assistive-technology conformance, end-to-end submissions/payments and administrative security remain unverified.

Recommended sequence

  1. Choose the visual direction; gather structured feedback on the homepage, directory, process and contact paths.
  2. Confirm brand assets/photo permissions and staff-approved policies, statistics, adoption details and contact routing.
  3. With owner access, preserve current custom CSS/code injection/settings and duplicate the affected pages. Do not assume Squarespace has a complete one-click restore. Keep global style changes off the live site until approved.
  4. Implement with Squarespace styles and blocks, retaining Shelterluv. Test mobile layout separately. Keep existing live URLs or establish deliberate redirects.
  5. Run owner-coordinated real application/contact/payment tests with clearly agreed test data and cleanup. No submissions were made during this audit.
  6. Publish only the chosen approved design, with rollback material retained, then verify primary paths and monitor owner-provided funnel data.
